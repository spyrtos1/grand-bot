**Le Refuge de Pierre** *(The Stone Refuge)*

## 2. Note d'intention (concept — English)

> Fortezza, the shelter I built for Silvae Fragmenta Vol. 4 in Ottati, asked a simple
> question: what does a body need from a wall on a mountain? In the Sancy, among the
> puys and the burons that have sheltered shepherds and their flocks for centuries, I
> want to ask it again, using the volcanic stone underfoot instead of the limestone of
> Cilento.
>
> The work is a low, one-person shelter built directly into the slope of a puy:
> dry-stacked phonolite walls, following the same technique — no mortar, only weight and
> fit — used in the burons themselves, so the structure reads as continuous with the land
> rather than placed on it. A single opening frames a fixed view down the valley. Set
> into the lintel of that opening is one worked block of Carrara marble, left visibly
> foreign to the site: the only cut, polished surface among rough local stone.
>
> The contrast is deliberate. Everything a shepherd needed to survive here for a season
> came from within walking distance. The marble is the one thing that did not — carried
> from another mountain, another country, a life spent as a sculptor rather than a
> herder. Set into the doorway, it marks the threshold between two ways a person can
> belong to a mountain: by staying, and by returning.
>
> Visitors can enter, sit, and look out through the frame. In winter, when the site is
> empty, the shelter continues to do what burons have always done: hold a fixed point
> against weather no one is asking it to fight forever.

### Motivations

> This project continues a body of work that began with Fortezza and that I want to keep
> building: site-specific shelters that use the vocabulary of stone construction — dry
> walling, load, threshold — to ask what a structure owes to a place versus what it
> brings from outside. Sancy's dry-stone buron tradition is close enough to techniques I
> already use in marble and fieldstone work in Italy that I can build this by hand,
> on-site, without heavy machinery. The festival's pairing of art with a working
> agricultural landscape, rather than a gallery or garden, is exactly the register this
> work needs.

## 3. Technical sheet

| | |
|---|---|
| **Materials** | Dry-stacked local phonolite/trachyandesite (source: to confirm with organisers — quarry offcuts or purchased locally); one worked Carrara marble block (lintel); timber roof structure (larch or douglas, weather-treated) |
| **Colours** | Natural stone greys/browns (local), pale grey-white (Carrara marble lintel), untreated silvered timber |
| **Footprint** | Approx. 2 m × 2 m |
| **Height** | 1.8–2 m |
| **Anchoring** | None required — dry-stone construction is self-stabilizing under its own weight (same principle as the region's burons); no foundations, bolts or concrete |
| **Weather resistance** | Dry-stone walling is inherently self-draining and wind-stable at altitude — it is the region's own traditional method for exactly this exposure. Timber roof sealed against UV and damp. |
| **Installation** | By hand; material delivery by small vehicle/trailer only, no crane or heavy plant needed |
| **Duration on site** | Estimated 8–10 working days to build; can be dismantled by hand in 2–3 days |
| **End of exhibition** | Stone returned to source/redistributed on site; marble lintel reclaimed by the artist; timber reclaimed or composted |

## 8. French version (machine-assisted — have a native speaker check before submitting)

### Note d'intention

> Fortezza, l'abri que j'ai construit pour Silvae Fragmenta Vol. 4 à Ottati, posait une
> question simple : de quoi un corps a-t-il besoin face à un mur, en montagne ? Dans le
> Sancy, parmi les puys et les burons qui abritent bergers et troupeaux depuis des
> siècles, je veux reposer cette question, avec la pierre volcanique du lieu au lieu du
> calcaire du Cilento.
>
> L'œuvre est un abri bas, pour une personne, construit à même la pente d'un puy : murs
> en pierre sèche de phonolite, selon la même technique — sans mortier, seulement le
> poids et l'ajustement — que celle des burons eux-mêmes, afin que la structure se lise
> comme un prolongement du terrain plutôt qu'un objet posé dessus. Une seule ouverture
> cadre une vue fixe sur la vallée. Dans le linteau de cette ouverture est scellé un bloc
> de marbre de Carrare travaillé, délibérément étranger au site : la seule surface
> taillée et polie parmi la pierre locale brute.
>
> Le contraste est voulu. Tout ce dont un berger avait besoin pour survivre ici une
> saison venait d'à côté. Le marbre est la seule chose qui ne vient pas d'ici — porté
> depuis une autre montagne, un autre pays, une vie de sculpteur plutôt que de berger.
> Placé dans l'embrasure, il marque le seuil entre deux façons d'appartenir à une
> montagne : en y restant, et en y revenant.

### Bio

> Spyros Anastasiou est un sculpteur chypriote installé à Carrare, en Italie, travaillant
> le marbre, la pierre, le bois, le bronze et la céramique, avec une pratique de land art
> grandissante. Son œuvre la plus récente, Fortezza, était un abri construit sur site
> pour Silvae Fragmenta Vol. 4 à Ottati, en Italie.
